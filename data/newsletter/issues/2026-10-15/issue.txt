Reports of tuberculosis with TNF inhibitors: what the FAERS signal does and does not show

A reporting signal is not proof a drug caused an event.

FAERS data as of 2026-07-30.

This is the first briefing, from the snapshot of 15 October 2026. There is no earlier snapshot to compare, so the lists below are the latest notices and the strongest prespecified signals in the current extract, not week-over-week changes. openFDA extract dated 2026-07-30. Myocardial Infarction and Cerebrovascular Accident cleared a reporting-odds bound only. The PRR is below 2, so those comparisons are not Evans signals and they are not the headline. PRR and ROR describe reporting. A higher ratio is a disproportionality signal. It is not an incidence rate.

There is no certainty that the reported event was actually due to the product. The information in these reports has not been scientifically or otherwise verified as to a cause and effect relationship and cannot be used to estimate the incidence of these events.

- TNF inhibitors · Tuberculosis
  Evans
  PRR 7.54 (95% CI 7.28–7.82) · ROR 7.56 (95% CI 7.29–7.84)
  Tuberculosis was reported more often than expected with TNF inhibitors than with all other FAERS reports (PRR 7.54, 95% CI 7.28–7.82). This is a disproportionality signal. The reporting odds ratio is 7.56 (95% CI 7.29–7.84). This meets the Evans screen: at least 3 reports, a PRR of at least 2, and a chi-square of at least 4. The ratio is not an incidence rate.
